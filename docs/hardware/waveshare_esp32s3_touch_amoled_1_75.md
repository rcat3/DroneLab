# The ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-1.75

Technical notes on the board every DroneLab unit is built on.  To build one, see the [Build Guide](../build/README.md).

The [Waveshare ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-1.75](https://docs.waveshare.com/ESP32-S3-Touch-AMOLED-1.75) is a 1.75" round AMOLED touch screen (466x466) on an ESP32-S3, with 8 MB of PSRAM and 16 MB of flash.  It has two microphones on an ES7210 audio ADC, a speaker amplifier with its own socket, a micro SD card slot, WiFi, Bluetooth LE, a QMI8658 motion sensor, a power button, and an AXP2101 power management chip that charges the battery.  Every [feature](../features/README.md) runs on it, including [voice effects](../features/voice-effects.md) in the [speaker model](../build/speaker-housing.md).

Use the bare board, not the -B version that comes in its own case.

## Board notes

- **Power:** the board runs from USB-C or from a 3.7 V LiPo battery on its MX1.25 connector (check the polarity before plugging a battery in), and charges the battery at up to 400 mA, which is gentle on either model's battery.  The firmware sets that, and also limits what the board draws from USB to 500 mA, which any USB port can supply: while the board's busy, the charger slows down to fit.  The speaker model's 2000 mAh battery charges from flat in about 5.5 hours, and runs the board for about 13.5 hours, or about 9.5 with [comms](../features/comms.md) on.  The low-profile model's 700-850 mAh battery takes about 2.5 hours and runs it for about 4.5-5.5.  Press `PWR` to turn it on, and hold it for 2 seconds to turn it off.  While it runs, `BOOT` switches the [voice effect](../features/voice-effects.md) on the speaker off and on.
- **Battery level:** worked out from the battery's voltage, along a curve the board learns (see [Battery Log](../features/settings.md#battery-log)).  The AXP2101's fuel gauge isn't used: without a profile for the battery it read up to 15% high halfway through a run.  Its charger status is used, for the charging bolt.
- **Brightness:** an AMOLED has no backlight, so the Brightness setting dims the screen itself.  Black parts of the picture are truly off.
- **Microphones:** the two microphones are mixed together.  Their gain is `MIC_GAIN_DB` in the board's `hal_board.c`.
- **Echo cancelling:** the board's audio chip also hears the speaker's own signal, so the firmware takes the speaker out of what the microphones pick up.  That lets the [voice effect](../features/voice-effects.md) go much louder before it feeds back: in the speaker model, every preset holds at 100% on the Effect Volume.  Without it, the first housings fed back at about 30%.  It's a small adaptive filter (NLMS), run a sample at a time so it adds no delay (`AEC_` settings in `hal_board.c`).  In the housing, the speaker reaches the microphones most strongly around 1.5 kHz, so that band is turned down a little after the echo canceller (`FEEDBACK_CUT_`), which lets it go a little louder again.
- **Voice through the mask:** the microphones hear you through the mask's speaking diaphragm, which booms around 250 Hz and muffles everything above about 2 kHz, where the consonants are.  The firmware turns the boom down and the treble up (`VOICE_BOOM_` and `VOICE_TREBLE_`), which makes the voice effects much easier to understand.  More treble than that mostly brings up hiss.
- **Speaker:** the microphones and the speaker share one audio clock, so sound is resampled to 32 kHz for the speaker, which is plenty for a small speaker.  `SPK_VOLUME` in `hal_board.c` sets how loud the board's amplifier goes, on top of the volume setting.
- **The speaker and the microphones:** the less of the speaker reaches the microphones, the louder the voice effect can go before it feeds back.  The first speaker housing, measured with music, seemed to need sealing inside: plasticine around the speaker's wire and behind the speaker cut the speaker in the microphones by about 5 dB, and what's left after echo cancelling by about 11 dB.  Measured since with the [Seal Test](../build/seal-test.md#whats-been-measured), the same board reads the same with the sealing and without it: nearly all of the speaker that reaches the microphones goes round the outside, through the mask.  So the speaker model isn't sealed inside, and the open-cell foam in the microphones' cell is there as a windscreen.
- **Antenna:** the 2.4 GHz chip antenna, for WiFi, Bluetooth and [direct mode](../features/comms.md#direct-mode), is on the back of the board, along the edge between `PWR` and `BOOT`.  Squeezed between the display and the battery, it loses about 20-25 dB, which keeps direct mode to about a room.  A 0 Ω resistor, R34, can be turned to switch it to the U.FL connector beside it, for an external antenna, but it's an 0201 part that needs a microscope.  See [Direct Mode Range](direct-mode-range.md).
- **Rotation:** in both housings the board is turned a quarter turn, with its USB-C port facing right, and the Rotation setting that puts the picture upright is 180 degrees.  The firmware starts with it set to that, and offers only 0 and 180 degrees: the housings' collars turn the whole unit upright, and turning the picture over in place leaves its internal RAM for the voice effects.
- **Images:** 466 pixels across fills the screen.
- **Not used yet:** the clock chip, and the GPS on the -G version.
